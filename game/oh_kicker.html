How Kicker works

Kicker is a 6-digit game: instead of chasing a jackpot, you choose a 6-digit number and win a fixed amount set by how you bet. A Straight matches all 6 digits in exact order and pays the top prize of $100,000 — a 1 in 1,000,000 shot — while a Box matches the same digits in any order for a smaller prize that comes up more often. Ohio draws it Mon · Wed · Sat at $1 (add-on) a play.

Digit games are the most transparent corner of the lottery: the odds are short, every payout is fixed and published up front, and there is no rolling jackpot to build. That also makes the house edge easy to see — the lottery keeps a set share of every dollar wagered no matter which number you pick, so it stays a negative-expected-value bet played for fun, not profit.

Our archive holds 2,247 Kicker draws going back to 2012, which powers the number-frequency statistics — the most and least common numbers, current hot and cold streaks, and the longest-overdue numbers. Those describe what has already happened; because each draw is independent, no number is ever truly ‘due.’

Most and least common Kicker numbers

Across 2,247 draws on the current 0–9 number pool (since 2012), the numbers drawn most often are 1 (1416), 7 (1386), 9 (1381), 8 (1352), 4 (1347), 5 (1338). The least often: 4 (1347), 5 (1338), 6 (1325), 3 (1316), 2 (1312), 0 (1309).

The commonest leads the rarest by about 8%, and that means nothing at all. Over this many draws ordinary random variation produces spreads of roughly this size — a fair game would almost never land every number on an identical count. No number is "due", and none of this predicts the next draw. We count only draws from the game's current number pool, because earlier draws used a different one and mixing them manufactures a pattern that was never there.
